# Internal research note — Lady Gaga's Dead Trademarks

**Strategic angle:** portfolio lifecycle — "dead" is a category with different kinds inside; slightly more technical than Series 3.
**Audience:** founders, brand managers, in-house counsel-adjacent readers.

## Scope discipline (Series 4 vs Series 3)
Series 3 asked "what does an abandoned application mean?" This carousel asks "what does a dead record mean?" and compares the two kinds present in the data: dead applications (42) vs. registrations that issued and later became inactive (10). No slide sequence or body copy is reused.

## IMPORTANT — status-wording limitation (flagged for human review)
The supplied exports label these records only as **"Dead."** They do **not** state the specific sub-status (abandoned vs. cancelled vs. expired vs. not renewed). For the 10 dead registrations, the slides therefore say they "left the register" / "ended" and that registrations "need ongoing maintenance filings" — deliberately avoiding the words *cancelled* or *expired* for any specific record. **Before publishing, pull TSDR status for the 10 registrations if you want to name the exact ending** (e.g., "cancelled under Section 8"). The carousel structure supports adding those labels without redesign.

## Other qualifications
- "That churn is health — not decay" is framed as a general portfolio principle.
- No claim that any specific registration was "not renewed" or "cancelled"; the maintenance-filing sentence is a general rule of law.

## Why it may perform
The dead-vs-alive HAUS LABORATORIES pairing is a genuinely surprising, verifiable hook that lands the "read the whole portfolio" lesson.
