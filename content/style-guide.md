# Vest Counsel carousel copy style guide

Distilled from the reviewed edits to the four Gaga trademark carousels
(August 2026). These rules govern all future carousel drafting.

## Punctuation

- **Never use em dashes (—).** Replace with a comma, a period, or two
  separate sentences.
- Prefer periods and commas over colons that chain clauses together.
- No rhetorical-question framing ("Filing? Do this. Searching? Do that.").
- Use typographic apostrophes (’) in display copy.

## Body copy

- **Shorter than you think.** One or two plain declarative sentences.
  Roughly 10 to 30 words; the earlier 20 to 45-word ceiling is now the
  upper bound, not the target.
- One idea per slide. State the lesson once and stop; no trailing
  qualifiers ("On its own, that is all it says") and no stacked detail
  clauses. Trust the reader.
- Cut secondary facts rather than compressing them into subclauses. If a
  fact matters, give it its own slide, the subheading, or the caption.
- Personalize where natural: "Gaga's portfolio," not "the portfolio."

## Subheadings (the bold line under the divider)

- Carry the data here: the stat plus a marquee example
  ("42 applications were abandoned, including BAD ROMANCE").
- The body below then interprets, in one sentence, what it means.

## Structure per carousel

- Cover (title only) → setup with the headline stat → three to five
  single-idea middles → practical conclusion → closing CTA.
- Titles: six words max, editorial, subject-referenced (for celebrity
  series, name the subject on every content slide).

## Closing slides

- Headline: a plain-language action statement ("Search before you
  brand"), not a themed restatement of the series.
- Subheading: a short direct invitation ("Reach out today").
- Contact block (phone, email, website) and the locked cat never change.

## Sourcing discipline (unchanged)

- Every featured mark, status, and figure verified against the supplied
  records before drafting; qualified attribution wording; unknown reasons
  stay unknown; abandoned ≠ cancelled ≠ expired; dead ≠ available.
